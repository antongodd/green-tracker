## Ofcom's two questions

Ofcom's guidance asks two questions, in order 🔎:

1. **Is it possible for children to use the service, or part of it?** The only way to answer "no" is to have
   *highly effective age assurance* (a real age check, such as a face age estimate, a photo ID match, or a bank or
   mobile-network check) that stops children getting in. **Ticking a box to say you're over 18 doesn't count**, and
   nor do rules that say "adults only" 🔎.
2. **If so, is the "child user condition" met?** It is if **either** a significant number of children use it, **or** it's
   *of a kind likely to attract* a significant number of children. "Significant" doesn't mean a large number or a
   large share of users; even a fairly small number can be significant, depending on the service 🔎.

If the answer to both is yes, the service counts as likely to be accessed by children, and it must do a children's
risk assessment and follow Ofcom's Protection of Children Codes. If the assessment isn't done at all, Ofcom treats the
service as likely to be accessed by children 🔎.

## Stage 1: can children use Green Tracker?

**Yes, in law.** What the app has:

- Sign-up needs **"I'm 18 or over and I agree to the house rules"** ticked (D84); the server refuses an account without
  it. Accounts from before were asked once ("One quick thing") before they could carry on.
- The **house rules** start with "18 or over only. No accounts for anyone younger."; the privacy page says "You must be
  18 or over to use Green Tracker."
- The **website** says 18+ at the top and "For adults 18+" at the bottom.
- **Report** has a "Looks under 18" reason.

These are honest and worth having, but none of them is a real age check: a 16-year-old can tick the box. So the
answer to stage 1 is yes, and stage 2 decides it.

### Is it the kind of service likely to attract a significant number of children?

Ofcom looks at things like what the service is about, how it looks, how it's promoted, and what's known about
children using similar services 🔎. For Green Tracker:

### Conclusion

**On the evidence today, the child user condition is not met:** there's no sign of child users, the service is a
small, invite-led diary for adult buyers with nothing public, and it isn't promoted at all, to children or anyone else.

**A warning, honestly:** this is a judgement, and it's the weakest part of this paperwork. The subject is one some
teenagers are interested in, the age check is only a tick, and Ofcom says even a small number of children can be
"significant" 🔎. Two things would make the answer safer:

- **Keep the evidence**: check reports for "Looks under 18" regularly, act on them (close the account; to-do 13), and
  note it here if it ever happens.
- **A real age check** (to-do 14) would turn stage 1 into "no" and end the question. It costs money (an age-check
  company charges per check) and adds a step at sign-up. Worth doing **before** opening Green Tracker to people beyond
  friends of friends.

### Look again if

Do this assessment again, before the change if it's a change to the app:

- **within 12 months** (by 3 November 2027) 🔎 (Ofcom expects it repeated at least yearly when the answer is "not
  likely");
- **any sign of a child using it** (a "Looks under 18" report, an email, someone you know);
- the app is **promoted** anywhere (social media, an app store, a forum), or the number of accounts **grows a lot**
  beyond people Anton's friends know;
- a change adds **public posts**, a public Feed, recommendations, private messages, groups, or anything game-like;
- Ofcom changes its guidance.

## Stage 3: a children's risk assessment

**Not needed now**, because stage 2 found the child user condition isn't met (Ofcom only asks for one when the
service is likely to be accessed by children 🔎).

If a later review finds it **is** met, it would be a big job: content about using drugs is on Ofcom's list of
**content harmful to children** (the "harmful substances" kind of priority content 🔎), and on Green Tracker that's
most of the content. In practice, the realistic answer then would be **a real age check that keeps children out**
(to-do 14), not making a cannabis diary suitable for children.
